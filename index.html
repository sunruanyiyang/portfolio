Construction and Classification of Minimum-Latency 5-Bit APN S-Boxes

Paper Submitted (Third Author)
Collaborator @ Chinese Academy of Sciences | May 2026 - Present
Research Problem: S-boxes serve as essential nonlinear building blocks in symmetric-key block ciphers. This research evaluates the offline construction and classification of minimum-latency 5-bit Almost Perfect Nonlinear (APN) permutation S-boxes under a fixed standard-cell gate delay model (NanGate 45 nm library) where area is secondary to speed.
Technical Approach: We decouple S-box permutation searching from heavy on-the-fly gate technology mapping. S-box evaluations are done by indexing precomputed scalar coordinate latencies based on input-variable permutation (IP) equivalence classes. Gate constraints are encoded into SAT formulas solved with kissat. We used Yosys, ABC, and OpenSTA to run netlist mapping and static timing constraints.
My Contributions: I implemented Shannon-decomposition heuristics in Python to split search trees and optimize the generation of Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF) constraints for exact synthesis of 5-bit boolean outputs. Additionally, I wrote a regression test harness ensuring that the synthesized Verilog netlists correctly preserved the Dillon permutation's logic boundaries.
Results: We established a complete, synthesized minimum-latency database covering all 5-input balanced nonlinear Boolean functions up to 150 ps. The framework successfully mapped, optimized, and verified S-box component designs with highly refined timing metrics.

Nonlinear Dynamics and Collective Synchronization

Ongoing Research (Undergraduate Researcher)
KE Research Group, Duke Kunshan University | Sep 2026 - Present
Research Problem: Analyzing phase locking behavior in coupled non-linear networks (like biological signals or power grids) to establish boundary thresholds where multi-phase systems transition into collective frequency synchronization.
Current Research Phase: I am conducting a structured literature review on mathematical oscillator models. This involves solving Kuramoto and Winfree network equations, examining mean-field approaches, studying phase coupling, and investigating bifurcation behavior resulting from network connection topologies.
Future Objectives: I plan to develop robust Python-based differential equation solvers to numerically simulate transition curves and analyze boundary parameters as network structures evolve.
